Responsible AI

Responsible AI in Uganda must start with context.

Responsible AI becomes meaningful when it is tied to real people, institutions and consequences.

Context comes before compliance.

A model can be technically impressive and still be harmful, irrelevant or impossible to govern in the environment where it is deployed. Responsible AI begins by asking who is affected, what data is being used, who can challenge a decision and who remains accountable when something goes wrong.

In Uganda, this also means paying attention to uneven connectivity, language, digital access, institutional capacity, privacy expectations and the realities of public and community services. A system that assumes perfect data, constant internet access or formal records may exclude the very people it is meant to help.

For Kampala Hub projects, responsibility should be built in from the start: define the users, identify risks, test assumptions, protect data, preserve human oversight and create a clear way to stop or correct a system when it causes harm.
